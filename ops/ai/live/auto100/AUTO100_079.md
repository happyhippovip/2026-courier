SOURCES_READ=Web search for "firmware mismatch", "PID tuning mismatch" and "unit-specific configuration" impact in robotics.
FACTS=Unit-specific mismatches (like mismatched firmware versions on I/O modules, or differing PID tuning parameters between visually identical robot arms) cause erratic execution, oscillations (jerky movement), or total communication failures. For VLA models, because they operate end-to-end, slight deviations in the physical dynamics caused by a PID mismatch can cause the observation history to drift out-of-distribution, leading to irreversible failure.
NEW_FINDING=While VLAs are often trained on large datasets across multiple robots (e.g. Open X-Embodiment), the community heavily relies on "Multi Error Proofing" or manual configuration management to ensure the deployment hardware matches the training hardware. There is a lack of a unified cryptographic ledger that binds the specific firmware version and low-level PID configurations to the VLA evaluation pass.
DUPLICATE_CHECK=First analysis focusing on low-level firmware and PID configuration as a mismatch vector.
WHAT_THIS_CHANGES=Further expands the Behavioral Identity Tuple. The evaluated "Identity" must include hashes of the low-level firmware and controller parameters (like PID gains) to prevent a model evaluated on a well-tuned arm from failing catastrophically on a poorly-tuned or differently-firmwared arm.
