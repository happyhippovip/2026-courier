### Executive Summary

In multi-agent governance frameworks, strategic decision councils (e.g. approving large treasury releases, voting on kernel upgrades, or adjudicating disputed execution faults) require unbiased, collusion-free voting. If agent votes are publicly attributable during deliberation, dominant or adversarial agents can exert economic or logical coercion, vote-buying, or targeted denial-of-service against dissenting agents.

This whitepaper defines an enterprise **Verifiable Shuffle & Anonymous Council Architecture** based on **ElGamal re-encryption mix-nets** and **Bayer-Groth zero-knowledge shuffle arguments**. Agents cast encrypted votes $e_i = \text{Enc}_{pk}(v_i, r_i)$. A sequence of independent mix servers permutes the ciphertexts and re-randomizes them with fresh randomness ($e'_j = e_{\pi(j)} \cdot \text{Enc}_{pk}(1, r'_j)$). The mix server produces an $O(\sqrt{N})$ argument proving that the output is an exact permutation and re-encryption of the inputs without revealing the permutation $\pi$ or opening any vote.

#### 1.1 Homomorphic Re-Encryption Invariant
- ElGamal ciphertext over cyclic group $G$ generated by $g$:
  $$e = (c_1, c_2) = (g^r, m \cdot pk^r)$$
- Re-encryption with fresh scalar $r'$:
  $$e' = (c_1 \cdot g^{r'}, c_2 \cdot pk^{r'}) = (g^{r + r'}, m \cdot pk^{r + r'})$$
- Decryption of $e'$ yields the exact same message $m$ as $e$, while the ciphertext representation $(c_1', c_2')$ is computationally indistinguishable from a random group element.
