# Important Side Thoughts — Durable Backlog

Purpose: preserve important strategic thoughts that may be lost when a chat, account, model, agent, or process changes. These are NOT automatically authorized actions. They are durable ideas/backlog items for future planning.

## Operating rule

`GEDANKE → EINORDNEN → SICHERN → NICHT STÖREN → HAUPTZIEL WEITER`

The current product goal remains primary. A side thought must not interrupt an active verified run unless it reveals a concrete safety issue or blocker.

## Strategic product thoughts

### 1. Keep upgrading the agent/Courier through real use
Courier is infrastructure, not the product goal. Improve it only when normal product execution exposes a concrete reproducible blocker or a clearly evidenced high-value improvement. Avoid speculative orchestration work. Desired loop:

`REAL PRODUCT GOAL → COURIER OPERATES → DEFECT/FRICTION OBSERVED → MINIMAL GENERAL IMPROVEMENT → VERIFY → RESUME SAME PRODUCT GOAL`

This lets the agent system become better through actual product work without turning agent-building into an endless side project.

### 2. Build a product that can eventually be sold
The software/product emerging from Courier-driven development should be evaluated not only for technical completeness but also for whether it can become a useful, coherent, sellable product.

Future product planning should therefore consider, when appropriate:
- clear target user and problem;
- coherent end-to-end user workflows;
- differentiated value;
- onboarding and usability;
- trust, privacy, safety and reliability;
- deployment/operability readiness;
- packaging/pricing/business-model hypotheses;
- evidence that users would actually want it.

This is strategic intent, NOT authorization to publish, deploy, contact customers, charge money, activate payments, purchase services, or spend funds. Those remain explicit human gates.

### 3. Revenue-first when choosing between equally valid product directions
When several product directions are all technically sound and consistent with the current goal, agents should prefer the direction with the strongest evidence-adjusted economic value.

Decision preference:

`USER VALUE → SELLABILITY → EXPECTED NET REVENUE → SPEED TO VALIDATION → COST TO BUILD/OPERATE → STRATEGIC LEARNING`

This does NOT mean chasing revenue at the expense of safety, product quality, trust, legality, or long-term viability. It means that among comparably safe and useful options, the system should prefer work that can reach real willingness-to-pay sooner and with lower cost.

The project should aim to become self-funding over time. Revenue generated by the product may later be reinvested into better tooling, subscriptions, model capacity, infrastructure, testing, reliability and product development. Any actual purchase, subscription upgrade, overage, payment activation, deployment or external sale remains a human approval gate.

### 4. Separate product progress from agent-system progress
Track two different kinds of value:
- PRODUCT_PROGRESS: capabilities and workflows useful to the eventual user/customer.
- AGENT_SYSTEM_PROGRESS: improvements to Courier/agents that make future product work safer, cheaper, more autonomous or more reliable.

Agent-system work should normally be justified by product execution, not replace it.

### 5. Durable handoff / crash recovery
Important state must survive chat/model/account/program failure. Canonical handoff records should contain evidence-backed YES/NO readiness flags, current goal, verified state, unresolved blocker, next safe action, fingerprints and test evidence. A new agent should resume from the last proven checkpoint rather than restart completed work.

### 6. Agent economics
Use the cheapest sufficient capability first. Gemini Flash / cheap deterministic tooling for routine work. Expensive specialist models only when expected information gain justifies them. Capacity is a resource, not a target to burn.

When evaluating a more expensive model/tool, compare expected information gain or risk reduction against its cost and the value of preserving capacity for later hard problems.

### 7. Human role
Desired end state:

`HUMAN IDEA/GOAL → COURIER → ROUTER → APPROPRIATE AGENT → EXECUTE → VERIFY → FINGERPRINT → STATE → NEXT SAFE TASK`

The human should increasingly provide goals and product judgment rather than manually routing workers, repairing queues, copying state between agents, or repeating context.

### 8. Commercialization should be evidence-driven, not automatic
Agents may autonomously analyze and prepare:
- target-user hypotheses;
- pain points and willingness-to-pay hypotheses;
- packaging and pricing options;
- cost-to-serve estimates;
- revenue model comparisons;
- product readiness gaps;
- onboarding and conversion improvements;
- low-cost validation plans;
- metrics that would prove or disprove demand.

Agents may use these analyses to prioritize internal product development when consistent with the active goal.

But agents must NOT autonomously perform external commercial actions without explicit human approval.

## Safety invariants for commercialization ideas

Ideas about selling the product are planning/backlog only until the relevant human gate is explicitly approved. No autonomous external commercial action is authorized. In particular, without explicit human approval: no publication/deployment, customer outreach, payment activation, purchases/upgrades/overages, account creation/rotation, wallet signing, real-money trading, or external sends.

## Future handoff rule

Before a major new phase, agents should read this file together with the canonical completion handoff and the product revenue/reinvestment strategy. They may incorporate relevant ideas into planning when they support the current goal, but should not automatically execute every idea in this backlog.
